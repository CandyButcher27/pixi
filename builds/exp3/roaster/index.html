N.º 03A torrefaçãoRua do Açúcar, Marvila

Uma Probat de 1962
e um caderno de capa preta.

Our roaster is a cast-iron Probat UG15 that spent forty years in a torrefação in Porto before we rebuilt it in 2017. It holds twelve kilos, heats slowly, and forgives nothing. We roast to taste, not to colour: every new lot gets four test profiles on a sample roaster, is cupped blind by the three of us on Monday, and only the winning curve goes onto the big machine.

We roast lighter than the Portuguese tradition and darker than Oslo. The goal is coffee that works both as a filter on a Saturday morning and as a bica at a zinc counter, which is how most of Lisbon actually drinks it. The Mezcla Alfama exists because our neighbours asked for it.

Green coffee comes from four importers we have visited, with prices paid to producers published on each bag. In 2025 we paid an average of 6.10 USD/kg FOB — 2.3× the C-market on the day of contract.

Chaff goes to the community garden on Rua Zófimo Pedroso. Jute sacks go to anyone who asks at the door.

N.º 05Visita

Bebe-se ao balcão, ao lado da máquina.

Morada
Rua do Açúcar 64,
1950-010 Lisboa

The café is the front of the roastery: eight stools, one Linea, a batch brewer and whatever came out of the drum that morning. On roast days you can watch the cooling tray from the bar. Pastéis come from Ferrugem, two doors down, until they run out.

Getting here: bus 728 or 759 to Rua do Açúcar, or train to Braço de Prata and a seven-minute walk. Tram 28 does not come this far east, whatever the guidebook says.
