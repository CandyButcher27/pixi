Mosca is one 15 kg drum, two roasters and a six-stool counter. We buy from eleven farms by name, roast lighter than Lisbon is used to and darker than Copenhagen would like, and sell every lot within fourteen days of the drop.

No blends named after moods. Each bag carries the farm, the altitude, the roast date and the batch number from the log, so you can tell us exactly which one you liked.

How a lot is roasted

Six steps, every batch logged.

  1. Sample roast and cupping

    Three 100 g samples at three development times. We cup them blind on Tuesday with whoever is at the counter, then pick one profile.

    Tue · 3 × 100 g

  2. Charge

    12 kg of green into a drum preheated to 210 °C. Lighter Ethiopians go in at 200 °C so the first minutes do not scorch them.

    0:00 · 210 °C

  3. Turning point and drying

    The beans bottom out around 88 °C, then climb. Full gas until they lose their grassy smell and go straw-yellow.

    1:20 → 4:50

  4. Maillard

    Gas comes down in two steps so the rate of rise falls steadily. This is where sweetness is made or lost.

    4:50 → 8:40

  5. First crack and development

    Between 1:40 and 2:10 of development, by ear first and by probe second. Filter lots drop earlier, espresso later.

    8:40 · 196 °C

  6. Drop, cool, rest

    Out onto the tray and cooled under four minutes, bagged with a valve, rested 5 days for filter and 9 for espresso before we brew it.

    10:30 · 205 °C
