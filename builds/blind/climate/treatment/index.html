Campaign 07 · Bayline LNG Export Terminal · Docket CP26-118

No permit
for Bayline.

A gas company wants to dredge 4.1 km of the Harrow Estuary and build a liquefied gas export terminal on the last unbuilt salt marsh between the river mouth and the city. If the state approves it on 18 December, it locks in 9.6 million tonnes of CO₂e every year until 2066. Forty-one community, fishing and faith groups are asking one thing: refuse the permit.

03 — What we are asking for

Four
demands.

Agreed by all 41 member groups at the October assembly. None of them require new law.

  1. Refuse the coastal permit on 18 December.

    The commission already has the authority: the application fails the state's own wetland-loss test and its 2035 emissions cap. A refusal is not a delay tactic, it is the legally correct outcome of the review it commissioned.

    To: State Coastal Commission, 5 commissioners

  2. Count the methane.

    Redo the climate chapter of the environmental review with upstream leakage included, as the 2025 state guidance requires. Leaving it out understates the terminal's footprint by roughly a fifth.

    To: Department of Environmental Quality

  3. Withdraw the $310 million tax abatement.

    Public money should not underwrite a fossil asset that the state's own plan says must be retired before its loan is paid off. Put the abatement to a recorded county vote.

    To: Harrow County Board of Supervisors

  4. Protect the marsh in perpetuity.

    Designate the 212 hectares as a tidal reserve, so the site cannot be offered to the next applicant. The shellfish growers, the tribe and the county parks office have already agreed to co-manage it.

    To: State Lands Board

04 — The one action

File a comment that counts.

The agency only counts comments that name a specific concern in the review. We have drafted one for you; edit it, add a sentence about why the estuary matters to you, and it goes straight into docket CP26-118 under your name. It takes about three minutes.

We never sell or share your details. Comments become public record with your name and town only.
