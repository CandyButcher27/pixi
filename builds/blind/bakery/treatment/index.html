From the night shift · Mara & Tom Ellery

Notes from the bench.

The starter is older than the shop. Mara brought it from her grandmother's kitchen in a jam jar in 2009, and it has been fed twice a day ever since — through a flood, a move, and one very bad August when the walk-in failed.

We buy flour from three mills we can drive to. That means the bread changes a little with each harvest: this year's Fenn Valley wheat is thirstier, so the country loaf went from 76% to 78% water in September.

We laminate croissants on Sundays and Wednesdays only, by hand, with a rolling pin and a cold marble slab. It is slower than a sheeter. It is also the reason they shatter the way they do.

Nothing on the counter is frozen and baked off. If you see a gap on the shelf at 11:00, that loaf is not coming back until tomorrow — ask, and we will hold one for you.
