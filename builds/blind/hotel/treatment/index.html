01 — The house

Four floors, one courtyard, no corridors.

A riad turns its back on the street. From Derb Sidi Bouloukat you see a blue cedar door and nothing else; inside, every room opens onto the courtyard galleries, and the light moves down the walls through the day. The 2019 restoration by Atelier Benjelloun kept the original carved plaster, re-laid 3,200 hand-cut zellige pieces from Fès and added nothing that needs a sign to explain it.

There is no lobby, no reception desk and no lift. Hassan or Meryem greets you at the door, and someone carries your bags up the stairs.

03 — A day in the house

The hours are set by the medina, not by us.

Times are for early November; the house shifts by about an hour across the year with the light. Nothing is compulsory, and nobody will knock before eleven.

  1. 06:31your room
    Fajr from the Mouassine mosque, forty metres from the door. Earplugs are on every pillow, most guests stop using them by the second night.
  2. 07:30courtyard
    Breakfast in the courtyard until 11:00 — msemen, baghrir with argan honey, amlou, eggs with cumin, orange juice from the four trees in season.
  3. 09:00the door
    Walks with Youssef, three mornings a week: the dyers' souk, the Mouassine fountain and the funduqs on Rue Sidi el-Yamani. 2 hours, 350 MAD per person.
  4. 10:00ground floor
    Hammam opens. Black soap, kessa glove, rhassoul clay, 50-minute sessions booked at breakfast.
  5. 13:00anywhere
    Lunch on request — salads, a tagine of the day, served wherever the shade is.
  6. 16:30roof
    Mint tea on the roof, poured from height as it should be, with sesame and almond briouates.
  7. 17:41roof
    Sunset behind the Koutoubia. The Atlas turns pink for about nine minutes.
  8. 19:30courtyard
    Dinner at the long table or in your room. Three courses, 520 MAD, wine list of 24 Moroccan bottles.
  9. 23:00the door
    The door is locked; the night porter, Abdelkader, opens it for you until dawn.

05 — Getting here

Cars stop at Bab Laksour. We walk you the last 300 metres.

The medina lanes are too narrow for cars. Our driver meets you in arrivals with your name on a card, drives to Bab Laksour, and a porter with a hand cart takes the luggage from there. Send us your flight number and we will track it.
