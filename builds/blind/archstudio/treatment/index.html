Rooms for a low northern light.

We are eleven architects drawing housing, schools and harbour structures for Copenhagen, in brick, timber and lime, built to outlast the people who commissioned them.

The practice

A city of courtyards does not need another object. It needs good rooms.

Copenhagen is built from the block: a ring of homes around a shared yard, the brick taking the weather so the courtyard can take the sun. Our work starts there, with the thickness of a wall and the depth of a window reveal.

We draw by hand before we model, and we stay on site until the last joint is pointed.

How a building happens here
  1. Walk the siteA week on the plot, at every hour, before a single line is drawn.
  2. Draw the section firstLight, height and the ground decide the plan, not the other way round.
  3. Choose one materialEach building gets one dominant material, used honestly and repaired easily.
  4. Stay until handoverThe architect who drew the detail is the one who checks it on site.
