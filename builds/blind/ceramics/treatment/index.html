Every piece below is listed with its clay, its firing, where it sat in the kiln and whether it is still in the studio. Nothing is made to order in batches; commissions are taken for one firing at a time.

HA-40-07 · moon jar · 46 × 44 cm
Featured · firing 40, March 2026

Moon jar with a broken ash line

Thrown in two halves from 14 kg of Jæren red stoneware and joined at the shoulder while leather-hard. The seam stays visible: a slight lean to the north side, where the jar faced the firebox.

It spent 84 hours in the front chamber, second row from the throat. Wood ash fell on the shoulder and melted into a runny green glass that stopped two-thirds of the way down, then flashed the bare clay below to the colour of a brick at dusk.

The underside carries a flame mark from the wadding — three small pale moons where it stood on shell and kaolin.

The kiln · noborigama, built 2013

Three chambers, four days, eleven people

The kiln climbs the slope behind the studio. Each chamber is stoked from the side once the one below reaches temperature. Friends work six-hour shifts; the log is kept by hand and copied here.

Glaze notebook · cone 10–12 reduction

Four glazes, all from the coast

The ash comes from the kiln itself; the iron from a bog-ore seam a kilometre inland; the feldspar from a quarry the studio shares with a stonemason. Recipes are by weight, sieved at 80 mesh. Use them.
