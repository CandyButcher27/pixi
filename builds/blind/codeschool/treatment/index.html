  1. 09:20

    Drop-off and warm-up puzzle

    One printed logic puzzle on every desk. Early arrivals get the hard one. Parents sign in at the door, not online.

  2. 09:30

    Ten-minute show-and-tell

    Two children demo what they built at home. Nobody is forced to; the queue is usually long.

  3. 09:40

    New idea, taught once

    One concept per week, taught in under twelve minutes with a live example. Loops, then conditions, then lists. Never three things at once.

  4. 10:00

    Build block

    Fifty minutes on this term's project. Teachers circulate with a rule: ask two questions before touching the keyboard.

  5. 10:50

    Snack, outside if dry

    Fruit and crackers provided. We hold nut-free and gluten-free stock; tell us on the booking form.

  6. 11:05

    Bug hunt

    Each child swaps laptops with a partner and fixes one planted bug. This is the part they talk about in the car home.

  7. 12:30

    Commit and collect

    Work is saved to the child's own folder and a one-line note goes to parents saying what changed today.

My child has never coded. Where do they start?

Everyone starts with a free taster Saturday on the rung their age suggests. By the end of the morning the teacher tells you whether to stay, move up or move down. About one in four children moves.

Do they need their own laptop?

No. We provide laptops for every session. From rung 4 we suggest a home computer for practice, and we keep a list of second-hand machines that work well under £150.

How much screen time is it, really?

Roughly 60% of a Saturday is at a screen. The rest is the warm-up puzzle, the taught idea on a whiteboard, the snack and unplugged games on rung 1.

What about children with SEND?

Around a fifth of our children have an EHCP or a diagnosis. Tell us what helps on the booking form; quiet desks, ear defenders and a visual timetable are standard.

Can I stay and watch?

On the taster Saturday, yes, at the back. After that we ask parents to leave, because children try harder things without an audience.

Is this exam preparation?

No. Children who go on to GCSE Computer Science tend to do well, but we teach for building things, not for papers.
