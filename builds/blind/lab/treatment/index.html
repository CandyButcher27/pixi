Matter that moves itself.

We study active fluids — suspensions of swimming cells, motor proteins and self-propelled colloids — and ask how order appears in materials that are never at rest.

Research — four open questions

What we are trying to find out

  1. 01

    When does collective swimming become turbulence?

    Dense bacteria form vortices with a preferred size. We map the transition as density and confinement change, and test which continuum models survive.

  2. 02

    Can a boundary steer an active flow?

    Patterned walls and soft obstacles rectify chaotic flow into net transport. We design the geometry and measure the pumping.

  3. 03

    How do defects carry information?

    Topological defects in active nematics are born, travel and annihilate. We track them in microtubule gels and ask what their motion encodes.

  4. 04

    What does it cost to stay organised?

    Order in a living material is paid for in energy. We estimate dissipation from fluctuations alone, without touching the sample.

Instrument

The 4D tracking rig

A light-sheet microscope we built in 2022 to follow every particle in a millimetre-sized drop, in three dimensions, for hours. Most of our data comes from it. Its designs are open.

Volume rate
48 volumes / s
Tracked at once
~120,000 particles
Resolution
0.6 µm lateral, 1.4 µm axial
